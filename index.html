How the peg board creates the result

The board is built as a triangle of staggered pegs, with each row wider than the one above it. A ball released at the top has a near-even chance of moving left or right at each peg, so the path is really a chain of small random steps. After it passes through the rows, it drops into one of the bottom pockets.

Because those steps are fifty-fifty at each point, most balls gather near the centre of the board and only a few reach the far edges. Statistically, the layout works like a Galton board, and the pattern of outcomes follows a binomial curve. The payout table simply mirrors that curve: central pockets are common and pay less, while edge pockets are rare and pay more.
